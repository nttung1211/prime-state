# Happiness According to Science

## 1. Pleasant Feeling

The moment-to-moment emotional "weather" of your inner life — the hedonic, in-the-moment experience of positive emotion. Through _hedonic adaptation_, positive feelings return toward a stable baseline after good events, often faster than we expect. This is what makes pleasant feeling the most fleeting of the three components — genuine and worth having, but not something that accumulates or holds on its own.

## 2. Life Satisfaction

A _constructed_ judgment rather than a stored fact — you build it on the spot, influenced by whatever is on your mind. It is essentially your perceived reality measured against two standards: your own expectations and the people around you. Because it is a comparison, it is relative and easily shifted, which is why improving circumstances don't reliably raise it (the hedonic treadmill).

## 3. Meaning

The eudaimonic sense that your life is worthwhile, composed of three interlocking facets:

- **Coherence** — your life makes sense and hangs together rather than feeling random.
- **Purpose** — where you are headed and what your life is for. It leans intrinsically toward the future.
- **Significance** — the felt verdict that your existence counts and would leave a hole if it vanished. Significance runs along two routes:
  - Mattering _to others_ (being needed, cared about by others)
  - Mattering _in the world_ (being able to make a difference, leave a mark, have an impact on the world around you)

Unlike the other components, meaning does not require pleasant circumstances and can even be forged through difficulty and sacrifice.

## The Key Insight

These three components can diverge, sometimes systematically. What raises **satisfaction** (ease, comfort, getting your needs met, being a "taker") is not the same as what builds **meaning** (effort, sacrifice, giving to others, being a "giver"). A life optimized purely for satisfaction may be light on meaning; a deeply meaningful life may be full of stress and sacrifice that depress moment-to-moment contentment.
