# Acetylcholine

> Note: The "spotlight that also marks circuits for rewiring" framing is a
> useful *model* that fits the evidence well — the attention-sharpening part
> is solid; the precise plasticity-gating story is cleaner in the model than
> in the messy literature. Confidence tags: [E] established · [R] reasonably
> supported · [M] model / extrapolation.

- Acetylcholine is the neurotransmitter of *focused attention* and
  *neuroplasticity* — not a "memory" molecule. Memory is the downstream
  *consequence* of what it flags, not what it does. [R]
- It has two separate roles: in the body it contracts muscle and slows the
  heart (peripheral); in the brain it regulates attention and rewiring
  (central). Only the central role matters here. [E]
- Released across the cortex during focus, it raises the *signal-to-noise
  ratio*: it strengthens the neurons handling what you attend to and quiets
  the rest. This is what "paying attention" physically *is*. [E]
- That same release flags the active circuits as *eligible for rewiring* —
  it is a primary gate for selective plasticity. The brain structurally
  changes mainly what it attends to. [R]
- Unfocused time, however long, builds almost nothing — no acetylcholine
  flag, no reason for the brain to rewire. The currency of learning is
  acetylcholine-marked focused time, not elapsed time. [M]
- The gate opens more readily under three conditions: *novelty/surprise*,
  *emotional/motivational significance* (where dopamine reaches over and
  helps open it), and *deliberate effortful focus*. [R]
- The third condition is the master skill: you can *manually* turn plasticity
  on for boring-but-essential things through voluntary effortful attention,
  rather than waiting for them to be intrinsically exciting. [M]
- Key distinction: it's not how *much* acetylcholine you have. It's how
  cleanly it's *aimed* and how reliably you can summon the focused state that
  deploys it. [M]
- Cognitive *strain* — the mild aversive effort of holding hard focus — is
  not a stop signal. It is the felt sign the plasticity system is engaged.
  Strain is to acetylcholine what the trough is to dopamine. [M]
- Plasticity is triggered most strongly at *errors and effortful struggle* at
  the edge of ability, not in the easy middle. Failure-at-the-edge is the
  trigger for change, not its absence. [R]

# How to Aim / Strengthen It

- Single-pointed focus only. Divided attention is near self-defeating: the
  suppression mechanism can't choose what to sharpen vs. quiet with multiple
  targets. Phone in another room — even a *visible* phone spends part of the
  suppression budget. [E reasoning]
- Spend your highest-focus window on your *hardest learning*, not shallow
  tasks. Focus capacity is a depletable budget; most people waste their
  freshest budget on email. Invert this. [R reasoning]
- Pre-aim the system: before a block, state in writing exactly what you'll
  focus on and want from it. The spotlight is partly directed top-down by
  stated goals; this is the aiming step, not a warm-up. [R reasoning]
- Train focus progressively like a muscle — it is limited, fatigable,
  trainable. Start at your honest current limit, extend over weeks toward
  ~90 min. Don't attempt marathons untrained and conclude you "can't focus."
  [M, gym-analogy]
- Recover with *low*-stimulation breaks (walk, window, eyes closed). A
  high-stimulation break (feed, novelty) doesn't restore the focus system and
  also drains dopamine baseline — worst of both. [R reasoning]
- Install novelty and stakes deliberately around dull-but-important material
  (active recall, teach it aloud, real deadline, an explanation you must
  deliver) — these pull the gate open without spending pure willpower. [R]
- Reinterpret strain as "this is working," and stay in it slightly longer —
  especially at the point of repeated near-misses, which is the strongest
  plasticity trigger. [M, but practically over-determined]
- Sleep is foremost: the consolidation that converts flagged daytime circuits
  into permanent structural change happens *during* deep slow-wave and REM
  sleep. A bad night fails to *bank* the plasticity you earned. [R]
- Supply the substrate: acetylcholine is synthesized from *choline*, which
  the body can't make enough of — egg yolks are among the richest common
  sources (a concrete reason the eggs in your plan earn their place). [E]
