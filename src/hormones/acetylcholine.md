# Acetylcholine

> Note: The "spotlight + plasticity flag" picture is a useful *model* that
> organizes solid evidence from attention and learning research — the
> molecular details are simplified. Confidence tags:
> [E] established · [R] reasonably supported · [M] model / extrapolation.

- Acetylcholine is the neurotransmitter of *focused attention* and
  *significance* — it flags which moments matter. It is NOT the "memory
  molecule"; memory is a downstream consequence of that flagging. [R]
- During focused attention it raises signal-to-noise: it sharpens the
  neurons handling what you focus on and quiets the rest. This *is* what
  attention physically is. [R]
- That same release marks the activated circuits as eligible for rewiring
  (neuroplasticity). The brain physically changes mostly what it attends
  to. [R]
- Consequence: unfocused time, however long, builds almost nothing. The
  currency of learning is acetylcholine-marked *focused* time, not time. [M]
- The gate opens more easily under three conditions: **novelty/surprise**
  [R], **emotional or motivational significance** (dopamine helps open it)
  [R], and **deliberate effortful focus** even on dull things [M].
- The third condition is the trainable master skill: you can manually turn
  plasticity on for boring-but-essential material via voluntary effort. [M]
- Key distinction: it's not how *much* acetylcholine you have. It's how
  cleanly it's *aimed* and how reliably you can summon the focused state. [M]
- Key reframe: cognitive *strain* is not a stop signal — it is the felt
  sign that the plasticity system is engaged. Strain is to acetylcholine
  what the dip is to dopamine. [M]
- Errors and struggle at the edge of ability are among the strongest
  triggers of plasticity — failure there is the trigger for progress, not
  its absence. [R]

# How to Use / Strengthen It

- Single-pointed focus only. Divided attention breaks the mechanism — the
  suppression system can't decide what to sharpen with multiple targets. [R reasoning]
- Spend your highest-focus window (for you: post-training, fed, matcha) on
  the single hardest learning task — not email or shallow work. Peak focus
  on trivial tasks is wasted premium fuel. [R reasoning]
- Pre-aim the system: before a block, state in writing exactly what you'll
  focus on and want from it. Goals direct the spotlight top-down. [M reasoning]
- Train focus like a muscle: it's limited, fatigable, trainable. Start at
  your honest current limit, extend over weeks toward ~90 min. Don't attempt
  marathons untrained. [R]
- Recovery breaks must be *low* stimulation (walk, window, eyes closed).
  Feed/novelty during breaks doesn't recover the system — it loads it while
  also draining dopamine baseline. Boredom is the recovery. [R reasoning]
- Install novelty + stakes around dull material to open the gate without
  pure willpower: change format, active recall, teach it aloud, real
  deadline, an explanation you must deliver. [R]
- Sleep consolidates the day's flagged circuits into permanent change
  (deep + REM). A bad night fails to *bank* the plasticity you earned —
  you can do everything right by day and lose it overnight. [R]
- Substrate: acetylcholine is built from choline, which must come from
  diet; egg yolks are among the richest common sources — a concrete reason
  the eggs in your plan do more than supply protein. [E synthesis · R diet]
