# Dopamine

> Note: "Baseline," "tonic dopamine," and the build-vs-drain symmetry are a
> useful *model* that organizes the evidence well — not a fully measured
> mechanism. Confidence tags: [E] established · [R] reasonably supported ·
> [M] model / extrapolation.

- Dopamine is the neurotransmitter of motivation and reward *anticipation*
  (wanting), not the pleasure of *having* (liking). [E]
- Baseline dopamine is your normal resting level. It determines how motivated
  and energized everyday activities feel. [M]
- When you experience a stimulus, dopamine rises above baseline. [E]
- After a rise, dopamine temporarily dips below baseline — felt as
  boredom, low motivation, or emptiness. The dip's depth roughly tracks the
  peak's height. [R]
- Faced with repeated sharp overstimulation, the brain *adapts* by reducing
  dopamine receptor sensitivity and/or number. [E]
- With a lower baseline and less sensitive receptors, normal activities feel
  less rewarding. [R]
- This drives cravings for stronger stimulation — a cycle of dependence. [R]
- Key distinction: it's not whether dopamine comes back down (it always does).
  It's how *sharply*, from how *high*, and what the rise does to the baseline.
  [M]

# How to Reverse / Build It

- Reduce repeated sharp overstimulation so the system can adapt back. Add
  *friction* (phone in another room, treats out of the house) rather than
  relying on willpower. [E reasoning]
- Exercise regularly — associated with higher baseline dopamine and improved
  receptor availability over time. [R]
- Sleep consistently; under-sleep itself lowers receptor availability, putting
  you in a low-motivation state before any choice is made. [R]
- Get morning sunlight — supports healthy dopamine regulation via the
  circadian clock. [R]
- Prefer effort-based rewards over instant gratification — plausibly
  baseline-building via demand→adaptation, like exercise. [M]
- Sequence matters: reward comes *after* effort, never as escape from it. For
  hard work, you must *close the loop* — define an endpoint, reach it, pause
  to register it. Effort without completion drains the baseline (burnout);
  effort with completion builds it. [M, but practically over-determined]
- Protect the first hour after waking — no feed before earned, gradual rises
  (light, movement). [R reasoning]
- Embrace boredom and the dip as signs of recovery, not failure. [R reasoning]
- Do one thing at a time; don't stack rewards (treat + show + scroll at once
  just builds tolerance faster). [R reasoning]
